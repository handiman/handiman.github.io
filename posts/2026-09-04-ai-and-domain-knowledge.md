Tools like Claude Code have genuinely changed how I work. Boilerplate, refactoring, exploring an unfamiliar codebase, drafting tests — a lot of that is faster now, and the quality bar for "good enough to start from" keeps rising.

But there's a real limit worth naming: these tools don't know your domain. They don't know why a business rule exists, which edge case actually matters to your users, or what "correct" means in your specific context. They pattern-match brilliantly on code — they don't understand your problem the way someone who's lived with it does.

Which is exactly why domain knowledge matters more, not less, right now. The people getting the most out of AI tools aren't the ones who trust them blindly — they're the ones who know their domain well enough to spot when the AI is confidently wrong, and to steer it when it's just guessing.

Bottom line: AI is a powerful tool, not a substitute for human judgment. Anyone who starts treating it as equivalent to human intelligence is setting themselves up for a bad surprise — usually in production.
